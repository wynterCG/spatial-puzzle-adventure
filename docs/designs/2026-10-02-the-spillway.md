# The Spillway

Design, 2 October 2026. Status: **blockout implemented**, not playtested. Generated by `tools/gen_spillway.py`; tested in `tests/spillway_tests.gd`. This is the third monument and the first candidate production level: a place first, a puzzle second. It introduces **rolling**: a rotor on a horizontal axis that turns the player over. It uses no Spatial Links.

The brief asked for:
- a place with a believable purpose;
- a strong world-down direction, so being upside down is dramatic;
- 2–3 transformable elements and 8–12 meaningful actions;
- position-dependent transformation, reuse, first-person discovery and preparation;
- legibility in both views, with clear colour regions.

## 2. Concept
*A sky reservoir you cannot enter from above. You get under it by letting its sluice drum roll you over, cross its water hanging beneath the weir arm, and leave down the inside of its own waterfall.*

## 3. Architectural fiction
A reservoir hangs in the void and collects condensation from the mist. An aqueduct from the west once metered its water to a city that is gone. Every moving part is a water-control device, which is why it moves:
- **The Sluice Drum** (coral) is a huge rolling gate set in the west wall: "closed" it is a bridge, "open" it stands on edge.
- **The Weir Arm** (teal) is a gauge arm that swings over the water from a turning **Crown** around the spillway throat.
- The Crown's raised lip is broken by a single **keeper's gate**. Water enters the throat through ports beneath the Crown, so the inside of the throat stays walkable beside the falls.
- **The Sanctuary** (amber), the keepers' observatory, hangs beneath the basin like an inverted tower, where the falls could be watched.

**The machines must read as infrastructure, not puzzle tiles:**
- **The Sluice Drum is a maintenance gate with built-in service decks.** It is a massive water gate whose axle is housed in the sluice house walls (visible bearings). A railed service walkway runs across its upper face. On its opposite face sit inspection rails and lamps, and a keel catwalk for inspecting the gate. Rolling it reads as operating the gate.
- **The Weir Arm is a measuring gantry.** Gauge rods dip into the water, depth marks run along its side, and railed decks cover both its top (survey deck) and its underside (inspection catwalk over the water). It swings to survey different parts of the reservoir.

**Two lamp languages, never confused:**

| | Path lamps | World-gravity elements |
|---|---|---|
| What | short rigid warm bollards, fixed **perpendicular to a walkable surface** (upside down under undersides, sideways on the throat stair) | chains, pendant lamps, drips and vines, always hanging toward **true world-down** |
| Says | "you can inhabit this surface" | "this is which way the world is oriented" |
| Look | squat, warm amber-white, no chain | slender, cool white, on long chains |

## 4. Isometric composition
Authored from the south-west, elevation about 32°, framed on the basin with room below it for the falls and the Sanctuary.
- **Start:** the ivory intake terrace on the incoming aqueduct (west), under a warm lantern.
- **Goal:** the amber Sanctuary hanging under the basin's east half. Its inverted spire glows far below.
- **Masses:**
  - the charcoal basin (72 m square) with teal water;
  - the Crown and Arm at its centre;
  - the ivory north gallery;
  - the sluice house and the coral Drum on the west wall;
  - the white falls column dropping from the throat into the mist.
- **What turns** is coloured: the coral Drum and the teal Arm and Crown.
- **Hidden:**
  - every underside (the landing's, the Arm's, the north gallery's wrap);
  - the throat's maintenance stair;
  - the Sanctuary's door.

The intended first thought: *"The light is under the basin, the arm rests at a gallery I can't reach, and nothing goes down."*

## 5. First-person moments
1. **The intake terrace.** You look across the void. The falls pour out of the basin's underside. The amber spire hangs below; the start is placed south-west so the gaze passes under the basin, and a test checks this sight line. Inverted lamps hang under the coral Drum's keel.
2. **The keeper's landing** (on top of the sluice house). The whole reservoir lies below: the Crown, the falls roaring into the throat, the Arm resting at the north gallery. Swinging the Arm here brings it 6 m below you: you cannot step down. Inverted lamps glow under it, over the water.
3. **The roll.** You stand on the Drum's deck and turn it. The sky wheels round and you come to rest **hanging under the sluice house**:
   - the water surface becomes a ceiling over your head;
   - the falls pour "upward" into the throat;
   - drips from the Arm fall away into your sky.
4. **Beneath the Arm,** carried over the water. Vines hang "up" from the north gallery's underside, and its walk wraps round the gallery's east end.
5. **On the Crown: the second deduction.** From the Crown deck you can read four things before turning anything:
   - the throat;
   - the stair on its east wall, lit by sideways path lamps beside the falling water;
   - the keeper's gate, the single break in the Crown's lip;
   - a matching **stair head** (an amber-marked landing on the throat rim) showing where the gate must come to rest.

   The intended thought: *"I need that opening over there."* A sight-line test pins that the stair head can be seen from the Crown deck.
6. **Down the throat and out underneath.** You walk vertically down beside the falls, round the bottom rim, and stand under the whole basin. The falls column drops into your sky. The Sanctuary's amber doorway stands upright ahead.

## 8. Intended solution
5 turns, 13 moves (solver-confirmed).

| # | Action | What the player knows / wants | Why it makes spatial sense | Reveals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cross the deck to the keeper's landing | the reservoir is the obstacle | the only open way | the whole reservoir; the Arm at the north |
| 2 | Swing the Arm west | "bring the bridge to me" | it is the obvious bridge | it docks 6 m below: **insufficient**, but inverted lamps glow under it |
| 3–4 | Stand on the Drum's deck and roll it over (2 turns) | "the lamps say undersides are walkable; the Drum carries me" | a rolling gate turns whatever stands on it | you hang under the sluice house, facing the Arm's underside |
| 5 | Step under the Arm | the earlier "useless" swing was preparation | the undersides line up | the water above you; the north gallery's vines |
| 6 | Ride the Arm north, hanging beneath | "it can ferry me" | the Arm carries its underside too | the north gallery's underside and its wrap |
| 7 | Wrap round the north gallery's end onto its top | changing sides | the walk visibly curls | you stand upright where the Arm first rested |
| 8 | Board the Arm on top and walk onto the Crown | the Crown was unreachable from above | the same Arm, used from the other side | the throat stair and the single keeper's gate |
| 9 | Ride the Crown back west | "the gate must be over the stair" | the Arm-west state **means something new from on top** | the gate meets the stair |
| 10–11 | Descend the throat and curl out under the basin | the stair goes down beside the falls | the falls show which way is down | upside down beneath everything, with the Sanctuary ahead |
| 12 | Walk into the Sanctuary | | | goal |

## 9. Key realisation
**The way under the reservoir is not down but over: let the drum roll you, and the arm that couldn't reach you from above meets you from below.**

## 10. Tempting wrong approaches (all recoverable)
1. **Swing the Arm to the landing** (step 2). It stops 6 m below. This teaches that heights matter, and the move is secretly the needed preparation.
2. **Roll the Drum while standing on the landing or terrace.** The bridge flips but you go nowhere. It teaches that a transformation carries only those standing on it.
3. **Roll the Drum one step.** You stand on a vertical wall with nothing joined. It teaches the Drum's states; turn it back.
4. **Ride beneath the Arm east or south, toward the light.** Nothing docks. It teaches that only the north gallery wraps.
5. **Reach the Crown with the gate facing south.** A raised lip, no way down. It teaches the single gate.

## 11. Perspective division
- **First person reveals:**
  - the inverted lamps that mark walkable undersides;
  - what is hidden beneath the landing and the Arm;
  - the north wrap;
  - the throat stair beside the falls;
  - the Sanctuary's door;
  - which way is world-down (falls, drips, vines).
- **Isometric reveals:**
  - the reservoir as one machine;
  - the Arm's reach and docking positions;
  - the Drum's states;
  - which way the keeper's gate faces;
  - where the goal hangs;
  - the 6 m drop between the landing and the Arm.

## 12. Spatial Links
**Not used.** The question here is "how do I get under or inside", and rolling, riding and the throat answer it physically. A link would skip the journey that is the level's identity. One possible later variant: a single link from the Sanctuary back to the intake, as a return path in the overworld. It is not part of this level.

## 14. Global orientation cues (world-down, always)
- **Falls:** the central column from the throat to the mist far below, plus overflow sheets spilling over the east wall.
- **Particles:** mist sinking slowly near the falls; drips falling from the Arm's underside and the basin floor.
- **Hanging things:**
  - vines hanging from the north gallery's underside and the basin's underside edges;
  - lamps on long chains below the galleries (plumb lines).
- **The water surface** is a flat plane that becomes a ceiling when you are inverted.
- **Upright far towers** with lit tops in the backdrop, and the incoming aqueduct's piers.
- **Rule:** none of these rotate with the rotors.
- **Strong in the blockout already,** not deferred to final art. Inversion is the level's emotional payoff, so the falls, drips, vines, chains and far towers ship in the first build.

## 14b. Reactive water (presentation only, no simulation)
The monument is a spillway, so its operations should visibly move water. Each effect is authored art bound to a rotor's state:

| Operation | Water response |
|---|---|
| Drum on edge (gate open, states 1/3) | a sheet pours through the gate into the basin, and the intake channel's flow strip brightens |
| Drum closed (states 0/2) | the intake channel pools at the gate, and the sheet stops |
| Keeper's gate over the stair (Arm west) | a thin overflow sheet runs over the gate's sill, a cue that it lines up with the throat |
| Always | the throat falls. More mist and drip emitters inside the throat and under the basin make the descent the loudest visual moment |

Audio (the water getting louder in the throat) waits for the audio pass. Spec §35 is not started, and there are no sound assets.

## 16. Playtest questions
1. Where did you start, and where were you trying to go? (Point in both views.)
2. Describe the reservoir: what is it for, and what moves?
3. Did you notice the hanging lamps? What did they tell you?
4. Why did the Arm not help the first time? When did that change?
5. When you rolled over, how did you know you were upside down? What showed it?
6. Did you plan before turning, or try turns at random? (Count the turns made.)
7. Under the basin: which way was "down"? Did it feel huge?
8. Did the Sanctuary always look like the end?
9. Show the opening isometric view without explanation. Can they say, roughly: "the light is under the basin, the arm rests at a gallery I can't reach, and nothing goes down"?
10. On the Crown: did you turn with intention ("the opening must go over there") or until something docked?

**Success moments to listen for:**
- reaching the underside: "the bridge was below me the whole time";
- on the Crown: "the same west position puts the opening over the waterfall now";
- afterwards, a spatial retelling: "I crossed the reservoir, rolled under the sluice gate, rode the water arm across, came back over it from above, and went down through the spillway to the pavilion."


## Implementation notes

- **Arm and Crown states.** The Arm is built pointing west at angle 0, so its states run west, south, east, north. It starts at state 3 (north). The keeper's gate sits opposite the Arm, as the abstract model has it.
- **The runtime confirms the design:**
  - every one of the 16 configurations joins exactly what section 7 says;
  - the minimum is 5 turns (16 actions counting each piece-to-piece walk);
  - unsolvable without the Drum, unsolvable without the Arm;
  - no turn is blocked by its sweep.
- **The keeper's gate** is a curl owned by the Crown: 90°, radius 0.5, with a 1.1 tail down the Crown's inner face. It docks the fixed throat stair at y −1.6, just below the Crown, so the turntable never overlaps the fixed throat. The stair ends in a fixed 90° curl round the throat's bottom rim; the east wall stops above that curl.
- **Docking tongues** (0.8 wide) on the Arm keep its sweep inside `sweep_tolerance` against the landing and the north gallery.
- **The start** stands at the intake platform's east edge. From mid-platform, the deck hides everything below about 9°, including the falls and the Sanctuary's spire. A test pins both sight lines.
- **Riding the Crown** is blocked while you stand at the Arm's tip, with the Arm turning toward the landing's face. The runtime says so ("inside the sweep"); the solution walks onto the Crown first, as section 8 says.
- **The Crown's hole has no curb.** A curb hid the stair head from its edge. The gate is marked by tall amber-capped posts instead. The stair head (an amber band and edges down the stair) is seen from the hole's edge with the gate still south, and a test pins this.
- **Reactive water** is state-bound art (`authoring/state_art.gd`):
  - a sheet pours through the open gate (Drum 1/3), and the channel pools against the closed gate (0/2);
  - an overflow runs beside the stair while the Arm is west.
- **The composition test:** from the opening overview, the player, Drum, Arm, falls and Sanctuary all project inside the frame and are not hidden behind boxes.

## Abstract solver

The model's states are (Drum state, Arm state, piece). Turns cost 1 and walks are free.
- **Minimum:** 5 turns, 13 moves, starting with the Drum at 0 and the Arm north:
  1. walk onto the deck;
  2. roll the Drum twice;
  3. step under the landing;
  4. swing the Arm west, step under it, ride it north;
  5. step under the north gallery, wrap onto its top, board the Arm;
  6. ride it west;
  7. descend the throat;
  8. walk out under the basin.
- **Necessity:** unsolvable without turning the Drum, and unsolvable without turning the Arm.
- **Where the keeper's gate sits:** opposite the Arm (gate east when the Arm is west). That is what makes the Arm-west state mean two different things. With the gate on the Arm's side the minimum is also 5; a quarter turn off gives 4 or 6.
- **Starting Arm direction:** north, docked at a gallery the player can't reach. Starting east would give 6 turns.
