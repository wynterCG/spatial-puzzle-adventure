# The Ring v3 and the authored isometric view

Design, 1 October 2026. Status: **implemented** (level revision 3). Supersedes the two-bridge Ring (revision 2).

Implementation notes: the tower is round (radius 2.4) so the inner arms (r ≥ 2.5) clear it on every turn. The interior curl sits west of centre, because a player on its vertical part stands sideways and must stay inside the cavity. A magenta guide line on the tower floor curls with it, so the route reads from the upper door. The balcony underside carries a warm light strip visible through the east slot. The authored composition is azimuth −45°, elevation 43.31°.

Monumental scale ([design](2026-10-01-monumental-scale.md)): rebuilt at ×4 from `tools/gen_ring.py` (revision 4). The dimensions below are at ×1.

Locomotion pass ([design](2026-10-01-locomotion-pass.md)): the tower doors widened from 0.8 m to 1.2 m, the width of the cavity behind them, and the entry and terrace jambs moved 10 cm outward, so the first-person eye never clips a wall. Walk regions and saves are unchanged.

## Why

Playing the two-bridge Ring showed two problems:

1. **First person had no job of its own.** The isometric view showed everything, so walking was just carrying out a plan made in isometric.
2. **The level was straightforward.** A solver over the abstract puzzle confirmed it: every single-rotor layout tops out around 9 steps over about 17 reachable positions, always shaped "ride, wait, ride". Hiding information alone cannot fix that; depth needs a second independent moving part.

The agreed direction is **"Perspective discovery and limited omniscience"**. First person reveals local spatial information: interiors, undersides, depth relationships and sightlines. Isometric is where the player reasons about topology and transforms it. It is deliberately not omniscient.

> **First person discovers why a configuration matters. Isometric lets the player act on it.**

## Part 1: authored isometric view (spec change, all monuments)

The isometric view becomes an **authored composition**, not an orbiting inspection camera.

- Each monument authors its framing: **centre**, **azimuth**, **elevation** and **base size**. New `ElsewhereLevel` fields: `overview_azimuth_degrees` (default 135, today's angle) and `overview_elevation_degrees` (default 43.3, today's angle); `overview_focus` and `overview_size` already exist.
- **Ground-relative orientation is kept**, along with the transport rules (§9.2, §9.3, §10). Arriving somewhere upside down shows the *same composition from the new gravity*. That changed view is the reward for moving, not something a camera drag can produce.
- **No free orbit.** Right-drag orbit is removed, and so is the manual yaw/elevation layer.
- **Limited zoom only:** ±20 % around the authored size (factor 0.8–1.25). It is for finding the avatar and reading joints, not for inspecting clues.
- **Home** resets the zoom. Tab still never resets the view.
- **Occlusion** is handled by authoring (composition, the existing player silhouette, and fades or cutaways where testing shows a need), never by camera freedom.
- Saves and undo stop storing yaw and elevation. Old snapshots containing them are still read; those keys are ignored.

Isometric controls become: `1–9` select structure, `Q / E` turn, wheel for limited zoom, `Home` to reset zoom, `Z` undo, `Tab` back to first person.

## Part 2: The Ring v3

### Pieces

The silhouette is unchanged: tower, ring band, wheel, entry outpost, and the terrace pier head with its light.

| Piece | Moves | Walkable | Geometry (radius r from tower axis; top level y = +1, underside level y = −1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ring** (band with one **ring arm**) | turns, 4 states, starts with the arm at W | arm **top and underside** | arm r 5 → 8, width 1.6; decorative band at r 6.5 |
| **Inner wheel** (hub with two half-arms) | turns independently, 4 states, starts with the top arm at E | E arm **top only**, W arm **underside only** | arms r 2.5 → 5, width 1.6, narrowing to 0.8 for the last 0.5 m at the tower so the turning sweep clears the doors |
| **Hollow tower** | fixed | interior slab joining its **upper and lower west doors** | round shell, radius 2.4 (decorative CSG, hidden interior), doors 1.2 m wide; slab: top path r 2.5 → x −0.7, 180° curl of radius 1, underside path back to the lower door; width 0.8 |
| Entry | fixed | top, W | as now |
| Balcony → terrace | fixed | underside, E, then curl up to the terrace | as now |

The **stair is removed**. Its job (changing sides) moves into the tower, out of isometric sight.

### Solution (12 steps; minimum confirmed by the solver)

Counting: the 12 design steps are 6 rotor turns + 6 walks. The solver (`tests/state_graph.gd`) counts 16 actions: the same 6 turns + 10 piece-to-piece walks, because two of the walks each cross three pieces (tower floor → curl → underside, and balcony → terrace curve → terrace). View changes and waiting are not counted in either.

1. From the entry: turn the **inner wheel** twice, so its top arm lines up behind the ring arm (W). There is now one continuous route: entry → ring arm → inner arm → upper tower door.
2. Walk in. Over the slab, curl around its far edge, back underneath to the **lower door**, upside down.
3. **From inside the tower:** turn the inner wheel twice, so its underside arm comes to the lower door.
4. Walk out under it and onto **the underside of the ring arm you came in on**.
5. Turn the **ring** twice while hanging under it: you are carried to E. Walk onto the balcony underside and curl up onto the terrace.

### Why it is deeper

- **Two rotors have to agree** before any route exists.
- **Preparation happens from specific places:** the inner wheel from the entry, the inner wheel again from inside the tower, and the ring while riding it.
- **Every piece has two roles.** The ring arm carries you in on top and out underneath. Each inner arm serves one direction of travel. The tower is both the side-changer and the waiting room.
- **Tempting dead ends**, each recoverable:
  - turning the ring toward the exit from the entry (you can no longer board);
  - entering the tower with only the top arm aligned, then leaving the way you came;
  - turning the inner wheel before entering;
  - stepping out of the lower door before the underside arm has arrived.
- **Necessity:** removing the tower, either inner arm, or either ring-arm face makes the puzzle unsolvable or shorter.

### First-person discovery layer

| Information | Why isometric doesn't give it | How first person gives it |
|---|---|---|
| The tower is hollow and changes your side | The shell is closed; only the two west door slots show | Looking through the upper door: the slab, its curl, the lower door |
| Which faces are walkable underneath (ring arm, W inner arm) | The composition looks down at your current ground | Looking up and down along the arms, or arriving upside down |
| The way out is underneath, at the east | The balcony underside faces away from the composition | A narrow **sightline slot** in the tower's east wall at underside eye level. From the lower door you see the lit balcony underside far to the east |

The level text supports this without solving it. Question: *"What do you see from inside the tower?"* Hints escalate from the sightline to the two-rotor order.

### Authored composition

The azimuth is chosen in testing so that the starting composition shows the entry, the ring arm, the tower's west door slots and the terrace light together. Coming out of the lower door re-presents the same monument upside down, from the new ground.

## Tests

All headless, launched from The Ring:

- connection table for all 16 configurations: no unintended joins, and the entry only boards the ring arm top;
- each dead end above is stuck and recoverable;
- the solution, waiting in both turn directions where it applies;
- carried local pose on both rotors;
- turning clearances at the tower doors and the r = 5 joint, in every configuration;
- Play From Here on the inner underside arm and on the tower interior;
- save, undo and checkpoint behaviour with two rotors;
- camera: orbit input is ignored, zoom is clamped to 0.8–1.25, Home resets zoom only, Tab never resets, and the authored azimuth and elevation are applied;
- the existing generic and fixture suites, updated for the removed orbit layer.

Verification also includes screenshots of the start composition, the view through the upper door, the sightline from the lower door, the upside-down composition, and the solved overview.

## Out of scope

- 90° snapped alternative views; add only if playtesting shows an unreadable relationship.
- Automatic per-selection shell fading; add only if the authored composition hides a selected rotor.
- New mechanics, levers or interactions.
- Final art.

## Playtest success question

Not "12 actions". Ask: *can the player explain why they entered on top, changed sides inside the tower, brought the underside arm to them, and only then rode the ring?* If they reached the end by cycling rotors at random, improve the clues (door read, sightline, composition), not the graph.

## Risks

- **Occluded selection.** 1–9 keys are fine today. If selection ever moves to clicking in the world, it needs a rule for picking partly occluded structures (the inner wheel behind the tower) without revealing the hidden interior.
- **No snap views.** Fix unreadable relationships with composition, fades or framing first; alternative viewpoints only if playtesting proves a need.

- Twelve steps is not depth by itself. The playtest question is whether players say *"I understand why"* or are just cycling turns. If they are cycling, improve the clues (sightline, door reads), not the step count.
- The turning clearance at the narrow tower doors and the r = 5 joint is tight (≤ 0.064 m overlap against a 0.08 m tolerance). Tests will catch it; the fallback is narrower necks.
- Removing orbit removes a comfort aid (spec §52). The composition, the player silhouette and ground-relative transport must carry orientation on their own; watch for this in the playtest.
