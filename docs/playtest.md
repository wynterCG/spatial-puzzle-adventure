# Revised opening playtest

Allow about ten minutes. This evaluates clarity and comfort, not speed. Technical checks are recorded in [validation.md](validation.md).

## First pass

Use Escape > Start a new journey if you want to restart; this replaces the current local checkpoint. Leave contextual guidance on and use in-game instructions before consulting the README walkthrough.

1. Follow the ivory path. Try walking sideways toward its boundary. Is it clear why the character stops instead of moving onto the wall?
2. Use the entrance control to extend the rounded walkway, follow it to the underside control and release the carrier brake. Return along the curve. Before boarding, predict what rolling the terracotta deck will do. Try it, observe the missing entrance connection, and bring it back.
3. Board and roll to the vertical landing. Can you locate your feet and predict which way walking will go after returning to third person?
4. Step onto the violet path and align the turning gallery from a safe position. Does the ground-relative overview make the accepted arrangement readable?
5. Walk through the covered gallery toward the light. Does it feel like walking through architecture? Note any visible pop, missing character, discontinuous wall or sudden change of view.
6. Before finishing, walk back through the corridor. Does the return work as expected? Then return toward the light and finish.

Record where you hesitated, which cue helped, and how many H hints you requested.

## Camera and recovery pass

- Compare follow distances 3, 5.5 and 8 while riding the deck and walking inside the gallery.
- Compare reduced motion and optional world horizon only if useful to you. Stop if uncomfortable; report the exact turn or crossing.
- Try restarting movement after a roll and orbiting at corridor entrances. Watch for camera clipping or an exposed spatial seam.
- Turn the carrier while aboard, disembark and use Undo. Confirm the structure and your position both return.
- Return to a checkpoint, switch first/third person and cross again. Accepted connections should survive all camera changes.
- Disable contextual guidance after learning the rules. Check whether the paving, mechanism and composed sightline remain understandable.

## Useful feedback

| Observation | Record |
|---|---|
| Location | Entrance, deck, landing, gallery or destination |
| Settings | Camera mode, distance, horizon, reduced motion |
| Action | Movement, orbit, roll, gallery turn or crossing |
| Expectation | What you thought would happen |
| Result | Confusion, discomfort or visible discontinuity |
| Helpful cue | Paving, landmark, ground-relative view, panel or hint |

Prioritize misleading boundaries, loss of orientation and exposed seams before expanding the campaign. Instrumented completion is not evidence that a first-time player understands the puzzle.

### Ground-relative overview check

- Turn while aboard: does the new surface read as the floor, and can you still locate the traveller?
- Turn an unoccupied mechanism: the view should keep its orientation.
- Inspect from inside the covered gallery: the architectural cutaway should expose the traveller and floor without revealing the spatial separation. Switching to traversal should restore the full walls and roof.
- Compare normal and reduced motion; report discomfort separately from puzzle difficulty.
- Check all four carrier states, undo and restarting the journey. Distinguish interesting spatial uncertainty from losing track of the character.

- At the gallery exit, identify the next walking surface and goal before entering. Does it read as one continuous level floor rather than two stacked structures?

### Contextual cutaway and upper-side camera

- With the traveller outside, verify that the complete roof and walls are present. Hover the roof, hold still, move away, and hover the UI: opening should be stable and limited to the module.
- Enter the gallery and switch to overview with the pointer elsewhere. The interior should stay open. Leave for the goal court and verify that it closes.
- Compare aligned galleries (one apparent passage) with unaligned galleries (independent reveals).
- While aboard, test all four carrier orientations in both directions. The camera should stay above your floor throughout the animated turns, with your head upright on screen. Check that the viewing angle stays on the same side relative to the deck, without a front/back swap or an extra overhead swing. Reverse walking direction and verify that the overview stays put.
- Compare reduced motion: geometry and camera should change together in one discrete turn. Test undo and checkpoint recovery afterward.

## Local controls and rounded paths

- Start a new journey. The carrier shows its engaged brake and refuses rotation. Can the player find the nearby lever without a spoken instruction?
- In third person, approach and use F. Does the rolled walkway extending into a curved ribbon explain where to go? Does its prompt disappear while it moves?
- Walk continuously over the rounded edge to the underside. Check camera comfort, held-forward input, reverse travel and side boundaries. Switch to overview midway and upside down: the character should still be seen from the same angle above their current ground.
- Operate the underside lever. Does the visible brake and feedback make its effect on architectural rotation clear? Return, board and turn the carrier.
- Undo each lever and a later carrier turn; recover a checkpoint on the underside. The route, controls, brake and feet must agree.
- Repeat with first person, short/long follow distance and reduced motion. First person is retained as a preference; it is never required. Reduced motion skips mechanism animations but walking around the curve still changes camera orientation.
- Complete the existing gallery puzzle after the new opening. Confirm the opening adds a useful relationship between views rather than feeling like an unrelated detour.

- Turn the gallery through all four orientations. Its floor and cover should remain one module, with no extra stationary slab projecting beneath it. Hovering should open the cover while keeping the floor visible; align and traverse it in both directions.
