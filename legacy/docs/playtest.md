# Positioning studies ? reasoning playtest

Allow 15?25 minutes. Use a fresh study from Escape. Do not explain the solution or read the specification's solution paragraphs to the player. Explain only movement, Tab, structure selection, Q/E and undo.

## Turning frame first when assessing the monument

Use **The turning frame** before Study 1 or The Lantern Fold: those puzzles teach the same path-choice dependency. Give only the controls and let the player explore in first person and isometric view. Keep H and the completion explanation optional.

- At arrival, ask them to point to their starting location and intended destination. Record whether the suspended lantern reads as the goal.
- Before turning, ask which surfaces they expect to connect and whether the player will be able to use that connection.
- If they board the upper passage, let the failed attempt happen. Ask what prevents crossing and where they think the useful path is. Do not name the underside route for them.
- If they align the empty frame first, observe whether they recognize the lost boarding connection and can recover by turning it back.
- After exploring below, ask them to identify that same passage in the overview. Record whether its hidden paving caused confusion.
- Before revealing the explanation, ask: **Could the frame be correctly aligned while you were in the wrong place? Why did you need to change paths before turning?**

Treat this as one positional insight. Success is a causal prediction and an understandable recovery, not just completion. A second role for the frame remains a possible later experiment; do not count extra turns as extra reasoning. Record first-person presence and camera comfort separately from solution understanding.

## Connected chapter comparison

Use **Chapter 1 / The far light** before the isolated studies. Follow the [courtyard observation protocol](first-chapter-plan.md#playtest-and-decision-record). Check the three assembly colours, architecture in both views, visible clearance obstruction, position before withdrawal, far-gallery ordering, recovery and landmark recognition. Ask for causal explanations before revealing the solution. These human outcomes are not yet validated.

## Complete puzzle - The Lantern Fold

Select **The Lantern Fold** from Escape. Test it before the isolated studies if measuring unaided reasoning; those studies teach its component dependencies. Explain the controls, but keep H optional.

- Can the player identify the lantern and distinguish the upper and longer underside paths?
- Does a failed upper-path attempt explain the missing reach, or just feel blocked?
- Can the player predict what docking will do to the Receiver's turning clearance?
- After docking too early, can they withdraw, prepare and return without restarting?
- Before revealing the completion explanation, ask: **Which actions needed to happen before docking? Which could happen in either order?**

Record hints used and recovery attempts. A full solution by repeated rotations does not establish understanding. Difficulty and play time remain unvalidated.

## Before each attempt

Ask the player to identify the goal, available walking paths and the structure that can move. Ask what they expect a turn to carry with it. Record their prediction before the action; do not correct it immediately.

## Study 1 ? Choose your deck

- Can the player distinguish the short and long paths on the carrier?
- If they rotate while aboard the short path, do they understand why the exit remains inaccessible?
- Do the curve and marked underside path suggest an intentional way to change position?
- After solving, ask: **Why did you need that path before the roll? What would rotating first change?**

## Study 2 ? The quiet landing

- Can the player identify a place that stays still while the carrier turns?
- Do they try remaining aboard? Can they explain why that carries them away from the useful connection?
- Is the fixed curve readable as the transfer to the other path's ground?
- After solving, ask: **Why did you step off? Why couldn't you simply board the second path from the start?**

## Study 3 ? Prepare the receiver

- Before manipulating either structure, ask which connection they would prepare and why.
- If they dock too soon, do the brass arms and rejection feedback explain the lost turning clearance?
- Can they recover by withdrawing the carrier without resetting?
- After solving, ask: **Which action consumed the clearance, and which action needed it?**

## Camera and accessibility

The user selected ground-direction transport (B) after the camera comparison. With manual orbit reset, test an occupied turntable and tilt: ask the player what moved, where the character ended up and which connection opened. Then introduce right-drag inspection and check that switching views preserves orientation. Record clarity and comfort for each player; the initial preference does not replace broader playtesting. [Decision and historical comparison](isometric-camera-experiment.md).

Start with first person, the chosen default. Walk through a curved gravity transition, stop and restart, look around, and switch to isometric view to recover orientation. Confirm that returning to first person preserves the occupied surface and heading. Compare reduced motion if useful. Stop if uncomfortable and identify the exact contact/turn. Third-person modes are optional experiments and do not need to be included in the primary playtest.

If comparing experimental third person: after returning to an ordinary platform, release all walking keys, orbit the camera substantially, then try W and D. They should follow the visible screen directions. Repeat on the underside. On the curve, stop/restart and freely look around: movement retains the transported route direction there. Check whether the local track cutaway makes the character readable without hiding too much of the route. A successful scripted loop is insufficient evidence that these general controls feel right.

## What to record

Record actions, predictions, hesitations, requested hints and the player's explanation before revealing the completion explanation. Completing by repeated turns is not sufficient evidence of understanding. A useful success is being able to predict and explain a wrong-order outcome on a replay. Separate clarity, enjoyment and camera comfort observations.

These are technical prototypes with unvalidated difficulty. The optional original gallery can be played afterwards for comparison, but is not part of solving the three studies.
