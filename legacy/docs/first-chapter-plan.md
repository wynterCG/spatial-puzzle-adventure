# First chapter plan

**Status: turning-courtyard revision implemented; human playtest pending.** [Chapter details](chapter-one.md) describe the actual geometry and checks. [Art direction](art-direction.md) records the revised design requirements. The earlier bridge/choice/relay chain is superseded; its underlying studies remain selectable.

## Introduction

Begin on the arrival terrace with the raised destination light visible. The outer curved gallery leads under the terrace. The folded access gallery introduces isometric manipulation and opening a physical connection. First person and isometric B remain the confirmed pairing.

Observe whether the player finds the curve, understands the changed ground and recognizes which assemblies move. Explain controls, not the solution. Avoid adding an unrelated introductory mechanism.

## Combined problem

Intended discovery: **the gallery that allowed entry now obstructs departure**. The player must walk onto the ring before withdrawing the access gallery. Staying aboard the gallery while folding it changes its orientation but cannot board the ring. Turning the empty ring early removes the boarding connection. Brass cheeks physically block either ring roll while the gallery is docked.

This remains true with unrestricted remote selection. Player position is an access condition, not an artificial range limit. Both ring roll directions are legal after clearing the gallery.

## Changed application and reward

The receiving gallery has the same joint on the opposite side and a different ground plane. Unfold it after the ring arrives; deploying it early consumes the final turning clearance. A player can reason about this before making a mistake, or withdraw it and recover. The far light is reached across the same architectural opening seen at arrival.

After completion, allow the player to look back and return. Returning reverses the useful role of the galleries: the far gallery becomes the access that must be withdrawn. This return is optional; it is not an extra completion chore. Test whether the departure arrangement itself provides sufficient independent application before expanding the chapter.

## Playtest and decision record

Use the chapter before the isolated studies with unfamiliar players. Explain WASD/mouse, Tab, selection, Q/E and undo. Keep H optional and record its use. Do not reveal the solution or require a failed attempt.

1. Ask what kind of place the overview suggests, and which structures can move. Can players identify each assembly by colour and silhouette under changed gravity?
2. Record first-person observations: does the curve feel like going underneath a terrace? Is the light recognized after the ring turns?
3. Ask for a prediction before the ring is turned. Can they see what occupies its sweep, and explain why their standing position matters?
4. If blocked, record whether the named obstruction and visible joint explain the result. Can they recover without restart?
5. Observe the far gallery without coaching. Do they transfer the clearance rule, or merely repeat turns?
6. Before revealing the completion explanation, ask: **Why must you leave the access gallery before withdrawing it? What happens if the far gallery opens early? What would returning require?**
7. Record hint level, undo/restart use, lost orientation, misread walkable edges, occlusion, discomfort and time. A successful sequence alone is not evidence of understanding.

Proceed toward a representative vertical slice only when the relationship is explainable and the space reads as a place. Otherwise revise geometry, framing or feedback. Do not default to more mechanics, switches or decoration. Broader theme, campaign length, lore and production scope remain open.
